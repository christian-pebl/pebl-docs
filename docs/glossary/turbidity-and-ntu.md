# What is turbidity, and what is NTU?

Turbidity is a measure of how cloudy water is, caused by particles suspended in it: sediment, plankton, organic matter, or anything else scattering light as it passes through. It is measured optically, not by weighing or filtering the particles out.

## How it's measured

A turbidity sensor shines light into the water and measures how much of it scatters back to a detector at a fixed angle (typically 90°). More suspended material scatters more light, which reads as a higher turbidity value. This optical, scatter-based method is what "nephelometric" refers to.

**NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Unit)** is the standard unit this reading is reported in. It is calibrated against a known reference suspension, typically formazin, at defined concentrations, so that a reading of, say, 40 NTU means the same thing across different instruments that have each been calibrated the same way.

## Why turbidity matters at a marine farm or restoration site

* **Light for photosynthesis.** Suspended sediment blocks light, which slows growth in seaweed and other photosynthetic species.
* **Smothering and clogging.** High turbidity events can settle out as sediment that smothers seabed habitat or clogs the feeding structures of filter-feeding shellfish.
* **A signal of disturbance.** A turbidity spike is often the first visible sign of a storm, dredging, construction, or other physical disturbance nearby.
* **A baseline and compliance metric.** Continuous turbidity logging is a standard input for environmental baseline surveys and BACI (Before-After-Control-Impact) monitoring designs.

## Calibrating a turbidity sensor

Turbidity sensors are typically calibrated with a two-point procedure: a zero (or near-zero) reference and a known-concentration formazin standard, to correct for drift in the specific unit's optics. PEBL's [GrowProbe](../growprobe/index.md) is calibrated this way over a 0 to 40 NTU range using a supplied formazin standard, see the [turbidity calibration guide](../growprobe/turbidity-calibration.md) for the full bench procedure.
